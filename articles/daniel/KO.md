# KO

I always found it interesting how, in my head, every letter has some kind of weird emotional attribute attached to it. It isn't anything too specific. It's just that, for example, when I see the letter “K”, I feel anxious, threatened for some reason. “O”, on the other hand, has a nice flow and a certain peacefulness to it. I like it.
I started thinking about why that might be. One possible explanation I came up with is that I associate letters with the names of people I know personally, specifically the first letters of their names.
My middle school classmate's last name is Olbert. I haven't seen him in over four years, I think, and still, whenever I see the letter “O”, I often remember how weirdly calm and easy my conversations with Thomas used to be. The funny thing is that I don’t even know what we used to talk about, I even forgot what Thomas looks like. I should call him.
Kristína, on the other hand, was always stressed out about something. It didn’t help that her nickname used to be “Kika”. So double the trouble. Every time I said, “Kika, it's going to be fine,” the letter “K” was uttered so often that it eventually became stained with all the icky emotions I was feeling back then. Really could have used any other word than icKy there…
I guess it’s not such a crazy concept. After all, that’s how names work, right? A couple of letters in succession, whose combination is meant to make you think of a specific person. I just find it interesting how they can hijack a letter in my mind and never let go. The logical explanation would be that the more time you spend with the person, more memories you make with them and more control they gain over the letter. But I don’t really think it’s that.
On that note, what would happen if I met another person whose name started with “K”. Would they have to compete with Kika for ownership of the letter? Or would they simply inherit everything she left behind? It seems a little unfair, being judged by a letter before you've even had the chance to introduce yourself. 
Maybe it’s the letters themselves, their angles, curves and shapes, that trigger something deep inside our brains. Something that has been forming throughout our entire lives, unique to each of us. All our experiences, traumas, fears and excitement somehow twisted together into one weird shape that we can draw on a piece of paper or produce with our vocal cords.
I guess I'll never really know. Maybe it's the people, maybe it's the shapes themselves, or maybe it's a bit of both. Either way, I find it strange that something as simple as a letter can carry so much meaning without us even noticing.  I wonder how many other things in my life have been quietly shaped by people I barely remember anymore. And whether, somewhere in someone else's mind, I've managed to ruin a perfectly innocent letter too.
