# A new sense of purpose

I had been asleep for almost four hours at that point, which was uncommon for this period of time. You had looked at me more than usual these past few months.
I remember the night you bought the tickets. And then the next night you returned them. It was weird. I think that was the starting point.
A couple of new faces appeared every day, from Monday to Friday. Faces whose names I had only known from text messages before. I came to know them pretty well: their features, backgrounds, rooms, and even pets. Some of them became so familiar that I could recognize them before their names appeared.
I remember the time you got the text. You spent a lot of time looking at two specific faces after that message. This time, they weren’t moving. They were only photographs. You kept going back to them, zooming in, as if there was something you had missed. Then you became really angry. Arguing with people online, sharing posts, trying to prove something. I had never seen you type so much, or delete so many things before sending them. I didn’t really understand what had happened, but I knew those two faces meant something to you.
Such a strange period of time. For most of my life, I felt like I was the thing keeping you away from faces. I rarely saw yours before that. I was always there, distracting you from whatever was happening around you. Now, suddenly, I had a sense of purpose, a duty. It was more than just keeping you entertained and making sure you met your deadlines.
I used to think my purpose was to keep your attention for as long as possible. Then, for a little while, I became the only way you could see the world outside your room.
Things are different now. You still use me every day, but the faces have slowly disappeared. You buy tickets again, and this time you actually use them. Sometimes you leave me asleep for an entire day.
And although I miss those days, I hope you never need me that much again.
